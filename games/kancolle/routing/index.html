Routing Explained: How the Compass Decides

The compass isn't just luck. Here's what it checks, a few real examples, and the ship type quirks behind most “why did it send me there?” moments.

01

Conclusion

The compass in KanColle isn't pure luck. At most branch points the game checks your fleet — ship types and counts, fleet speed, line of sight (LoS), equipment and a few others — and only falls back to random when the rules say so.

Reading the wiki's routing page, two details explain a lot of “why did it send me there?” moments: the check happens when you press advance, not when you sortie, and the game uses its own ship type groups, which don't always match what the ship card says.

An analysis-based guide: this guide is based on our analysis of the routing rules page on the Japanese KanColle wiki. It describes what the data shows and is not a guarantee of results.

What the Compass Checks

FactorWhat it means
Ship types and counts“5+ battleships and carriers → node E”. The most common rule, and the reason many maps limit heavy ships.
Specific shipsShips tied to the historical battle a map is based on, e.g. Shoukaku and Zuikaku together.
Number of shipsSome nodes route 6-ship fleets differently from smaller ones.
Flagship typee.g. a light cruiser flagship with destroyers.
Line of sight (LoS) scoreYour fleet's recon score must meet a threshold. When it does, you see a scout plane animation.
Fleet speedFast, Fast+ or Fastest fleets often skip whirlpools or bypass other rules entirely.
EquipmentDrums, Daihatsu landing craft and radar are the usual ones — usually counted by how many ships carry them, not how many items.
Nodes visitedWhether you've passed (or unlocked) a certain node.
Event-only factorsCombined fleet type, gimmicks, ship tags, gauge state and difficulty.

05

Practical Tips

  • The fleet is checked at each advance. If you send a ship home mid-sortie (escort retreat), the next branch sees the smaller fleet.
  • Equipment usually counts per ship. Two drums on one ship counts as one ship with drums. Spread them out.
  • Speed is often the strongest rule. A Fast+ or Fastest fleet can override battleship and carrier limits on some maps. See Fleet Builder for speed.
  • LoS uses a formula, not the number shown on ships. The wiki calls it Formula 33. Use a fleet-planning tool to calculate it rather than adding up stats by hand.
  • “Random” can be weighted. Some random branches lean one way depending on the fleet, like 4-1's 70% example.
06

FAQ

Is routing ever pure luck?

Some branches are random, but many are fixed by your fleet. When a branch is random, the fleet can still change the odds.

Why do I keep missing the boss on 2-4?

2-4 has several routing checks. See the 2-4 guide for the fleet the rules point to.

What's the scout plane animation?

It means your fleet met the LoS check at that node. If you don't see it where you expected, your LoS score was too low.

Do levels matter for routing?

Ship level isn't a routing factor, but it affects the LoS score through the formula.
