2-4 Guide: Okinoshima Sea Area

2-4 is the first real wall for new admirals. Here's a fleet that clears it, why the compass keeps sending you the wrong way, and the air-power number that matters more than you'd think.

01

Conclusion

Fleet I cleared it with: 2 battleships, 2 carriers, 1 heavy cruiser, 1 light cruiser — all around level 40. It took me about five sorties.

What matters most: enough fighter power to beat the enemy carriers on the way (over 84 for Air Superiority), retreating whenever a ship is heavily damaged, and a composition the compass likes.

Why it's hard: every route makes you fight an enemy carrier group, and a fleet full of battleships and carriers is sent the long way — usually four battles before the boss.

From my own runs: my early attempts mostly ended in a retreat on the node just before the boss, because someone was heavily damaged. The compass also kept sending me away from the boss until I changed my lineup to the one above. The biggest surprise was how much air control mattered.

Standard or light carriers? According to the routing data on the Japanese wiki, at the second battle (node F) a fleet with no light carrier and one destroyer or fewer is sent to node A, which ends the sortie without reaching the boss. A light carrier makes F lean strongly toward the boss side instead. If you keep ending runs at an item node after your second fight, swap one standard carrier for a light carrier.

Equipment

  • Carrier fighters (艦上戦闘機, Kanjō Sentōki; shown as 艦戦, Kansen). The highest enemy fighter power before the boss is 56, so you want more than 84 for Air Superiority (航空優勢, Kōkū Yūsei) and more than 168 for Air Supremacy (制空権確保, Seikūken Kakuho). Check your total with the Air Superiority Calculator.
  • Main guns on the battleships and heavy cruiser — the biggest ones each ship can carry.
  • Radar (電探, Dentan) on a few ships if you have spares. Battleship-and-carrier fleets pass a whirlpool (うずしお, Uzushio) that drains fuel, and radar reduces how much you lose.
  • Optional: Damage Control (応急修理要員, Ōkyū Shūri Yōin) saves a ship from sinking once. You don't need it if you follow the retreat rule.

Not available here: 2-4 doesn't allow support fleets or land-based air squadrons, so everything rides on your six ships.

03

Step by Step

  1. 1From the home port (母港, Bokō) open Organize (編成, Hensei) and set up the fleet above. Put a sturdy battleship in slot 1 — the flagship can't sink.
  2. 2Load the carriers with fighters first, then check that your fighter power is over 84 with the calculator.
  3. 3Resupply (補給, Hokyū) everyone and Repair (入渠, Nyūkyo) any damage. Use an Instant Repair bucket (高速修復材, Kōsoku Shūfuku-zai) if you're in a hurry. Never sortie with a damaged ship.
  4. 4Tap Sortie (出撃, Shutsugeki), pick sortie again, then World 2 → 2-4, named 沖ノ島海域 (Okinoshima Kaiiki). Don't confuse it with 2-5, 沖ノ島沖 (Okinoshima-oki) — the names differ by one word.
  5. 5Choose Line Ahead (単縦陣, Tanjūjin) against surface fleets. Against the enemy carrier groups, which come in a ring formation, Line Ahead is still a reasonable default; Diamond (輪形陣, Rinkeijin) trades firepower for anti-air.
  6. 6After every battle, check each ship's damage. If anyone is heavily damaged (大破, Taiha), choose Retreat (撤退, Tettai). Otherwise choose Advance (進撃, Shingeki).
  7. 7Skip night battle on normal nodes (追撃せず, Tsuigeki sezu). Take it at the boss (夜戦, Yasen) if the enemy flagship is still alive — heavy cruisers and battleships hit hardest at night.
  8. 8Win the boss battle to clear the map.

Why the compass sends you off course

The compass (羅針盤, Rashinban) isn't fully random — your fleet composition sets the odds at each fork. Node letters below follow the Japanese wiki; the game itself doesn't show them.

ForkWhat the routing data saysWhat it means for you
After the 1st battle (B)3+ battleships and standard carriers → node C (a whirlpool)You'll lose some fuel. Radar reduces the loss.
After the 2nd battle (F)A light carrier, or 2+ destroyers, leans toward the boss side (J). No light carrier and ≤1 destroyer → node A, the sortie ends.The most common reason a battleship-heavy fleet never sees the boss.
Node J4+ battleships and carriers → L; exactly 3 → M; 2 standard carriers → MBoth lead toward the boss, but via different fights.
Node LExactly 4 battleships and carriers → M (an extra carrier fight). A light cruiser plus 2 destroyers, or ≤2 battleships and standard carriers, → straight to the boss.A 2 BB + 2 carrier fleet fights at M before the boss, which is why heavy damage before the boss is so common.

The wiki's route data also shows a shorter path: with 1 battleship + 1 standard carrier + 1 light carrier (3 big ships), J goes straight to M and then the boss — one fewer fight. I haven't tested that myself. There's also a fixed two-battle route for 1 heavy cruiser, 1 light cruiser and 4 destroyers, but it needs well-leveled, well-equipped light ships, so it's not ideal for a first clear.

04

Common Mistakes

  1. 1Ignoring air power. Whichever route you take, you'll fight at least one enemy carrier group led by a flagship Wo-class carrier. Lose the air battle there and you reach the boss already damaged. This was my biggest surprise — once I had enough fighters, the trip got much easier.
  2. 2Advancing with a heavily damaged ship. A heavily damaged ship other than the flagship can sink if it's hit again, and a sunk ship is gone for good with its equipment. Retreat, repair, and try again.
  3. 3Treating the compass as pure luck. If you keep ending at the same wrong node, the fix is almost always composition — see the routing table above. Changing my lineup is what made routing reliable for me.
  4. 4Stacking every battleship and carrier you own. Four or more big ships sends you through node M for an extra carrier fight, so raw power isn't the whole answer.
  5. 5Forgetting to resupply between attempts. Ships low on fuel or ammo fight worse, and the whirlpool takes fuel too.
  6. 6Running out of resources. A battleship-and-carrier fleet is expensive. Keep expeditions running while you repair — the Expedition Requirements page shows what you can send.
05

FAQ

Do I need max-level ships?

No. I cleared it with every ship around level 40. Composition, fighters and retreating at the right time matter more than raw level.

Why do my runs end after the second battle?

At that fork (node F), a fleet with no light carrier and one destroyer or fewer is sent to node A, which ends the sortie. Add a light carrier, or two destroyers.

How much fighter power do I need?

The strongest enemy air before the boss is 56, so aim for more than 84 (Air Superiority). More than 168 gets Air Supremacy. The boss fleet has no carriers.

Can I bring support fleets or land-based air?

No — neither can be used in World 2.

What does clearing 2-4 unlock?

It's one of the two maps (with 3-2) you need to clear before 3-3 appears, and 2-5 — an Extra Operation map — comes after it in World 2.

Is there a reason to come back after clearing it?

Yes. A quarterly quest, 沖ノ島海域迎撃戦 (Okinoshima Kaiiki Geigekisen), asks for two S-rank boss wins here and rewards a Mamiya (間宮) food supply ship item. 2-4 is also the only regular map with a node that hands out Instant Repair buckets.

Which formation should I use at the boss?

Line Ahead (単縦陣, Tanjūjin). The boss fleet is surface ships only — up to three battleships, also in Line Ahead — so anti-air formations just cost you firepower there.

Why do my attacks miss so often?

Many of the enemy escorts on this map are late-model destroyers (後期型, Kōkigata), which dodge a lot. Every miss gives the enemy another turn to shoot back, which is part of why damage piles up on the way to the boss. Higher-level ships and better guns help.

What do the damage labels mean?

小破 (Shōha) light, 中破 (Chūha) moderate, 大破 (Taiha) heavy. Only heavy damage means you must retreat.
