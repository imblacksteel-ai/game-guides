Social Policies and Policy Cost

Every branch and policy from the game's data, and a calculator that uses Unciv's own cost formula — including how much each new city adds.

01

Conclusion

Unciv has 10 social policy branches, each with five policies plus a bonus for completing the branch. Two pairs lock each other out: Piety vs Rationalism, and the three ideologies Freedom, Autocracy and Order.

Reading the cost formula in the source, policies get expensive fast: the base cost goes 25, 34, 62, 108, 173, 256… (before the game rounds down to a multiple of 5) and every extra city adds 10% on small and medium maps. That's the real trade-off between going wide and adopting policies quickly — the calculator below shows it for your game.

An analysis-based guide: this guide is based on our analysis of Unciv's Policies data file and the cost formula in its source code. It describes what the data shows and is not a guarantee of results.

Formula from the game's code: (25 + (adopted × 3)2.01) × difficulty × game speed × (1 + map modifier × (cities − 1)), rounded down to a multiple of 5. Map modifier: 10% per city on Tiny–Medium, 7.5% on Large, 5% on Huge. Policies and wonders that reduce policy cost aren't included.

FAQ

Why did my next policy suddenly get more expensive?

You probably founded or annexed a city. Each non-puppet city beyond the first raises the cost by 10% on Tiny–Medium maps.

Do puppet cities raise policy cost?

No. The formula only counts cities that aren't puppets.

Can I take both Piety and Rationalism?

No. Each is only available before you adopt the other. The three ideologies also exclude each other.

Is this the same as Civ V?

The structure follows Civ V: Gods & Kings, but the formula here is Unciv's own code.
