Which Civilization Should You Pick?

Not a tier list from play — a grouping of all 34 civs by how their ability actually works, to help you choose.

01

Conclusion

There's no single best civilization in Unciv — but the data makes it easy to see which abilities pay off in every game and which depend on the map. For a first game, the safest picks are the ones whose bonus works from turn 1 with no setup: Egypt, Babylon, Rome and Korea.

This is a reading of each civ's ability text, not a ranking from play. Map, difficulty and victory type change what's strongest.

An analysis-based guide: this guide is based on our analysis of the ability and unique-unit data of all 34 civilizations in Unciv's Nations file. It describes what the data shows and is not a guarantee of results.

How to Use This

  • New to the game: pick from the “works from turn 1” row and play your usual way.
  • Know your map: if you're playing an archipelago, Polynesia or Denmark; lots of hills, the Inca; many city-states, Greece or Siam.
  • Going for a victory type: the AI's preferred victory for each leader is on the Civilizations page — a hint at what each was designed for.
  • Look at unique units' abilities, not just stats: 24 of 49 unique units have the same base stats as the unit they replace; their edge is in the abilities listed on the Civilizations page.
04

FAQ

Which civ is best for Deity?

The data alone can't say. On high difficulties the AI's head start matters more, so bonuses that compound early (production, science) tend to be the reliable kind.

Which civ is best for a cultural victory?

Civs with culture or wonder bonuses (Egypt, France) fit, but remember that each extra city raises policy cost by 10% on small maps.

Are any civs weak?

Map-dependent civs can look weak on the wrong map. That's a property of the map, not the civ.
