Opening Guide: The First Turns

Not a build order from experience — what the game's own numbers say about the first turns, and why.

01

Conclusion

This isn't a build order from experience — it's what the game's data and code say about the first turns. Put together, they point to a simple opening: Scout → grow to size 2 → Settler, a Monument early for culture, and a policy choice based on how many cities you plan to have.

The reasons are all in the numbers below: a Settler needs 2 population, excess food turns into production while you build one, policies get 10% more expensive per city, and each city costs at least 4 happiness.

An analysis-based guide: this guide is based on our analysis of Unciv's data files and source code (no first-hand play experience). It describes what the data shows and is not a guarantee of results.
02

An Opening the Data Supports

  1. 1Scout first (25 production). It ignores terrain movement costs and is your best way to pop ancient ruins early (see below).
  2. 2Grow the capital to size 2. A Settler can't be built below 2 population.
  3. 3Build a Settler (106 production). While it's being built, excess food is converted to production, so a food-rich capital builds it faster.
  4. 4Get a Monument (40 production, +2 culture) to start earning policies. The first policy costs 25 culture.
  5. 5Pick your first branch by city count. Tradition boosts the capital and gives 4 free Aqueducts on completion; Liberty gives +1 culture in every city and a free great person on completion. Since every extra city adds 10% to policy cost, Tradition suits a few big cities and Liberty a wider empire.
  6. 6Watch happiness before each new city. A new city costs 3 happiness plus 1 per citizen; each different luxury gives +4.

FAQ

Is this the best opening?

It's the opening the numbers support, not a tested build order. Map, civilization and difficulty all change what's best.

Should I go Warrior first instead?

On higher difficulties barbarians can enter your land from turn 1, so a defender can matter more there. The data doesn't settle it for every map.

How many cities should I build?

There's no fixed answer, but the costs are clear: each city raises policy cost by 10% and tech cost by 5%, and costs at least 4 happiness.
