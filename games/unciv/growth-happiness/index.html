City Growth and Happiness

The real formulas behind growth and happiness, read from Unciv's code — with a full food table.

01

Conclusion

A city needs 15 food to grow from size 1, then 8 more per size plus a small extra: 15 + 8 × (size − 1) + (size − 1)1.5, rounded down. Each citizen eats 2 food, and only the surplus counts toward growth.

Happiness is the brake. Every city costs 3 and every citizen 1, while each different luxury gives 4. Once your empire dips below zero, growth drops by 75%; below −10, cities stop growing and units fight 33% weaker.

An analysis-based guide: this guide is based on our analysis of Unciv's growth and happiness code (CityPopulationManager.kt, CityStats.kt) and GlobalUniques.json. It describes what the data shows and is not a guarantee of results.

05

Practical Takeaways

  • Spread your luxuries. A new kind of luxury is worth +4; a second copy of one you have is worth nothing until you trade it.
  • Each new city costs at least 4 happiness (3 for the city, 1 for its first citizen) before buildings.
  • Annexing is expensive. An annexed city starts at −5 and doubles its citizens' unhappiness until it has a Courthouse; a puppet avoids the extra penalty.
  • Growth slows gently, not sharply. Size 10 → 11 needs 114 food and size 20 → 21 needs 249 on Standard speed.
06

FAQ

Why did my city stop growing?

Check empire happiness: below −10 growth stops completely. Also check that the city has a food surplus after its citizens eat 2 food each.

Do specialists eat food?

Yes, 2 food each like any citizen.

Does difficulty change unhappiness?

Yes — your unhappiness is multiplied by 40% on Settler, 60% Chieftain, 75% Warlord and 100% from Prince up.
