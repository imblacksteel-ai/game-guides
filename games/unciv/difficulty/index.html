Difficulty Levels Explained

What actually changes at each level, from Settler to Deity — checked against the code that applies each number.

01

Conclusion

Reading Unciv's difficulty file next to the code that uses it, the levels work in two halves. Settler, Chieftain and Warlord make things cheaper for you (Settler halves your unit, building and policy costs). Prince is the neutral baseline for costs and growth — those are all 100%, though the AI already gets a little extra unit supply (+20%).

From King upward, your costs and happiness stop changing — only your combat bonus against barbarians keeps shrinking. The rest of the difficulty comes from the AI: cheaper units and buildings, faster city growth, a bigger unit cap, extra starting units and free techs. On Deity, AI units and buildings cost half.

An analysis-based guide: this guide is based on our analysis of Unciv's Difficulties data file and the source code that applies it. It describes what the data shows and is not a guarantee of results.

04

Which Level to Pick

  • Learning the game: Chieftain or Warlord. You get cheaper costs and more happiness, and barbarians are kept out early.
  • A fair game: Prince. Costs and growth are even on both sides; the AI gets +20% unit supply and trades techs with other AIs from Prince up.
  • Challenge: King and above. Your costs no longer change, so each step up mainly means a stronger AI economy and a bigger AI head start.
05

FAQ

Does Deity make my research slower?

No. Your tech cost is 100% from Warlord up. Deity's difficulty is the AI's advantages.

Why is the AI weaker on Settler than me?

On Settler the AI needs 160% food to grow and pays 175% for units and 160% for buildings and wonders, while your costs are halved.

Is game speed included?

No. Game speed multiplies costs separately on top of difficulty.

Do these apply to city-states?

City-states have their own rules (for example, they pay 150% for units), so the AI rows here are about major civilizations.
